Phase I: Primitive Resource Conversion (Chapters 1–5)

Concrete, stepwise early-game crafting and resource rules to stabilize spawn and first night operations.

Chapter 1 — Silvicultural Wood Processing

  • Harvest order: Fell trees from base → top to avoid floating trunks.
  • Conversion rule: 1 log → 4 planks: craft only what you need early to conserve inventory. :contentReference[oaicite:1]{index=1}
  • Stick economy: 2 planks → 4 sticks; plan sticks for torches, tools, scaffolds.
  • Charcoal fallback: Smelt excess logs into charcoal if no coal found nearby.
  • Replant loop: Collect saplings; plant immediately in rows of 2× spacing for fast regrowth.

Chapter 2 — Primitive Tool Progression

  • Crafting table unlock: 4 planks → Crafting Table (3×3 grid).
  • Tool ladder: Wooden pick → Stone pick → Iron pick as soon as iron is available.
  • Resource ROI: Upgrade to stone tools immediately after 3–4 cobblestone to improve efficiency.
  • Tool kit: Carry 1 extra pick, a shovel, and an axe for expedition resilience.
  • Repair & conserve: Use anvils or crafting to combine partially used tools; keep spare materials for emergency crafting.

Chapter 3 — Thermal Energy & Smelting

  • Furnace craft: 8 cobblestone → Furnace; prioritize furnace near base.
  • Fuel hierarchy: Coal > Charcoal > Wooden items; lava bucket is single highest single-item burn time.
  • Smelt batching: Stack smelts (full stacks) to reduce repeated loading overhead.
  • Smoker/tripwire: Use a Smoker for meat (twice as fast) to speed food production.
  • Auto-smelt tip: Funnel into a chest + hopper for continuous unattended runs once automated.

Chapter 4 — Luminance Grid & Early Safety

  • Torch recipe: 1 stick + 1 coal/charcoal → torches (use liberally for light). :contentReference[oaicite:2]{index=2}
  • Spawn safety: Keep base interior at light level ≥ 8 to prevent hostile spawns.
  • Lanterns: Craft lanterns for permanent, aesthetic light points (torch + iron nuggets).
  • Path lighting: Light all approach paths at 6–10 block intervals to prevent stumbling mobs.
  • Emergency cache: Store a stack of torches in a chest for quick runs.

Chapter 5 — Chronological Skip Devices (Beds & Respawn)

  • Bed craft: 3 wool + 3 planks → bed; sets respawn and skips night.
  • Location choice: Place bed inside protected compound, not exposed to open threats.
  • Multiplayer use: Color beds to assign player respawn mapping in teams.
  • Backup plan: Keep a secondary bed in a safe remote outpost for expeditions.
  • Nether note: Beds explode in the Nether and End — do not use there for sleeping (except as a weapon tactic).

Phase II: Bio-Organic & Nutritional Logic (Chapters 6–10)

Food & farming: set up renewable food sources, storage, and emergency nutrition patterns.

Chapter 6 — Bread, Grain & Vertical Farms

  • Bread recipe: 3 wheat → bread; prioritize automated wheat plots near base.
  • Vertical layout: Stack 3–5 layers with water flow channels to maximize small-footprint yields.
  • Bone meal cycle: Use composters and bone meal to push rapid growth in emergencies.
  • Seed economy: Reserve 10–20 seeds per plot for replanting runs.
  • Storage: Keep one chest dedicated to baked goods + one to raw ingredients.

Chapter 7 — High-Saturation Horticulture

  • Golden carrot: High saturation food for sprint & boss prep; keep a reserve stack for raids.
  • Carrot farms: Villager-farming or manual rows with water canals is efficient.
  • Rotation: Rotate between bread and golden carrots for balanced saturation and recovery.
  • Preservation: Use chests labeled for snack / combat / expedition food for quick grabs.
  • Automation: Use observers + pistons on carrot plots for semi-auto harvest into hoppers.

Chapter 8 — Apple & Golden Apple Use

  • Apple sources: Leaf decay from logged trees; use shears for leaf collection when needed.
  • Golden apple: 8 gold ingots + apple — keep for emergency high-risk encounters.
  • Curing synergy: Golden apples required in curing zombie villagers for permanent discounts.
  • Stack policy: Keep 2–3 golden apples in your hotbar for boss fights.
  • Crafting economy: Use gold reserves primarily for golden apples only when needed.

Chapter 9 — Protein & Smoking

  • Smoker priority: Smoker cooks meat twice as fast; place adjacent to animal pens.
  • Meat farms: Breeding pens with looting/enchantment synergy to maximize yields.
  • Saturation stacking: Cooked meat > raw meat; always cook before long expeditions.
  • Auto-feed: Setup hopper-fed smoker into chest for sustained supply lines.
  • Emergency rations: Carry a stack of cooked meat for deep-cave or Nether trips.

Chapter 10 — Suspicious Stew & Effect Management

  • Suspicious stew: Bowl + mushrooms + flower → temporary effect; use situationally for early buffs.
  • Effect selection: Match flower type with required status effect (e.g., dandelion for saturation variants).
  • Short duration: Use stew just prior to specific tasks needing boosts (mining, combat).
  • Preserve bowls: Reclaim bowls to minimize raw-material waste.
  • Stack plan: Reserve small stack for exploration; do not rely solely on stew for continuous buffs.

Phase III: Metallurgical & Defensive Engineering (Chapters 11–15)

Mid-game: armor, weapons, and defensive systems for raids and PVE control.

Chapter 11 — Shields, Armor & Impact Dampening

  • Shield craft: 6 planks + 1 iron ingot → shield for projectile mitigation.
  • Armor sequence: Leather → Iron → Diamond → Netherite; upgrade when materials become stable.
  • Enchant priority: Protection/Projectile/Mending for chest & helm; Mending is highest value long-term.
  • Repair policy: Use anvils + XP to repair important pieces; keep a spare set for emergencies.
  • Combat stance: Crouch+shield timing reduces explosion/arrow damage in raids.

Chapter 12 — High-Tier Weaponry

  • Sword vs axe: Sword for rapid hits; axe deals burst damage but slower.
  • Netherite upgrade: Use smithing table to upgrade diamond gear to Netherite for durability and knockback resistance.
  • Enchant choices: Sharpness/Smite/Bane depending on target mobs; Looting on swords for drops.
  • Bonus tools: Carry a crossbow with fireworks for Elytra boost and a bow for ranged fights.
  • Loadout: Primary melee, secondary ranged, backup tools in off-hand or chest slots.

Chapter 13 — Projectile Ballistics & Mobility

  • Bow craft: 3 sticks + 3 string → bow; use Enchantments (Power, Infinity/Multishot) as needed.
  • Crossbow role: Higher damage, faster projectile; pair with fireworks for Elytra propulsion.
  • Arrow economy: Create skeleton farms and fletchers for arrow/emerald trades.
  • Mobility tech: Use potions (Swiftness/Leaping) for tactical repositioning during fights.
  • Backup ammo: Carry at least 64 arrows or Infinity bow when exploring endgame content.

Chapter 14 — Excavation Explosives

  • TNT craft: sand + gunpowder → TNT; use cautiously and with planned blast radius.
  • Mining pattern: Use controlled TNT blasts for bulk clearing; always set anchors to avoid base damage.
  • Redstone timing: Use repeaters to chain blasts safely for staged explosions.
  • Backups: Keep world backups when experimenting with large TNT projects.
  • Safety shells: Create blast-proof shields or remote detonation rooms for testing.

Chapter 15 — Scaffolding & Rapid Vertical Movement

  • Scaffolding craft: bamboo + string → scaffolding; fast vertical builds and collapse cleanup.
  • Use-case: Ideal for tall builds, temporary towers, and repair work.
  • Safety: Always carry 10–20 scaffolds for emergency escape during builds.
  • Other tech: Use water elevators with soul sand and magma for controlled movement.
  • Storage: Keep scaffolding stacks near construction zones for speed.

Phase IV: Redstone Logic & Industrialization (Chapters 16–20)

Automation foundations: signals, timing, detection, item flow, and storage compression.

Chapter 16 — Signal Conductivity & Basics

  • Redstone dust: Transmits power; place on solid blocks for continuity.
  • Torch inverter: Redstone torch inverts signal — core for logic gates.
  • Line limits: Redstone carries for 15 dust segments before repeaters required.
  • Compact runs: Use slabs and comparators for tight builds.
  • Debugging: Break the line to test subsystem behavior stepwise.

Chapter 17 — Repeaters, Delays & Timing

  • Repeater craft: 3 stone + 2 redstone torches + redstone dust → repeater for delay & extend.
  • Delay tuning: Use repeater ticks to sync harvesters and pistons.
  • Clocks: Create stable clocks with repeaters for periodic automation.
  • Pulse shaping: Use comparators + hoppers for pulse-length control.
  • Power saving: Use observer-triggered pulses instead of always-on clocks where possible.

Chapter 18 — Observers & Update Detection

  • Observer craft: 6 cobblestone + 2 redstone + 1 quartz → detects block updates.
  • Crop automation: Observers detect plant growth to trigger pistons/harvesters.
  • Machine triggers: Use observers for tile-change events like dispenser outputs.
  • False positives: Plan for multi-update noise (use delays or filters).
  • Placement: Orient observers to face the block you want to monitor directly.

Chapter 19 — Hoppers & Item Flow

  • Hopper craft: 5 iron + chest → hopper; moves items at 2.5 items/sec.
  • Locking: Use comparator + redstone to lock hopper flow for sorting.
  • Sorters: Filters using comparators funnel items into dedicated chests.
  • Overflow: Plan overflow chests to prevent item loss during bursts.
  • Maintenance: Place access points for clearing jams (trapped chests or sideways hoppers).

Chapter 20 — Storage Compression & Shulker Logic

  • Chest vs barrel: Barrels work with hoppers and save space; chests are easy for item frames.
  • Shulker boxes: Portable storage that preserves contents when broken — use to scale inventories. :contentReference[oaicite:3]{index=3}
  • Shulker sorters: Use comparators to detect full boxes and swap with pistons for rotation.
  • Labeling: Use item frames or signs for quick identification of chest contents.
  • Automated compaction: Drop items through crafting/compressor systems to convert into storage blocks (e.g., ingot → block).

Phase V: Alchemical & Arcane Mastery (Chapters 21–25)

Brewing, enchanting, XP infrastructure and how to prioritize arcane upgrades for longevity.

Chapter 21 — Brewing Infrastructure

  • Brewing stand: Blaze rod + cobblestone → brewing stand; place near nether resource storage.
  • Base potion: Awkward potion from Nether wart → base for most brews.
  • Ingredient staging: Keep blaze powder, nether wart, redstone, glowstone, and sugar organized in labeled chests.
  • Batch brewing: Use multiple stands in parallel for expedition-ready potion stacks.
  • Fuel: Blaze powder fuels brewing stands — secure blaze rod sources from Nether.

Chapter 22 — Fire Resistance & Utility Potions

  • Fire resistance: Awkward + magma cream → Fire Resistance (critical for Nether ops).
  • Extend vs enhance: Redstone extends duration; glowstone increases potency where applicable.
  • Combat potions: Strength, Regeneration, and Healing are priority for boss encounters.
  • Storage: Keep potion racks or bottles in labeled crates by type and duration.
  • Distribution: Station dispensers for potion lanes in PvE arenas for quick use.

Chapter 23 — Enchanting Table & Bookshelves

  • Crafting: 4 obsidian + 2 diamonds + 1 book → Enchanting Table.
  • Max level: Place up to 15 bookshelves spaced one block away to reach level 30 enchantments. :contentReference[oaicite:4]{index=4}
  • Lapis resource: Lapis Lazuli + XP required per enchant; secure mining runs for lapis reserves.
  • Book economy: Enchant books for trade/backup; Librarian trades are a source of high-value books.
  • Enchantment policy: Prioritize Mending, Unbreaking, and Fortune for long-term gains.

Chapter 24 — Anvils, Grindstones & Enchanted Books

  • Anvil uses: Repair and combine enchanted gear; note increasing XP costs with repeated use.
  • Grindstone: Removes enchantments and repairs via combining non-enchanted items.
  • Book handling: Combine enchantments on books first, then apply to gear to reduce costs.
  • Library station: Keep a set of enchanted-book backups for rapid gear recovery after death.
  • Cost planning: Use grindstones to remove unwanted enchants before re-enchanting to save levels.

Chapter 25 — XP & Experience Farming

  • XP sources: Mob farms, smelting, breeding, and mining are core XP inputs.
  • Spawner farms: Convert monster spawners to XP farms with drop/half-heart mechanics for safety.
  • AFK policies: Build safe AFK spots with mob collection funnels for steady XP inflow.
  • Use cases: Prioritize enchant and repair cycles — keep XP reserves above 30 for quick enchant access.
  • Trade synergy: Villager trading and smelting can also be tuned for XP and resource throughput.

Phase VI: Village Economics & Trading (Chapters 26–30)

Villager mechanics, workstation control, and economic loops that generate rare items and enchanted books.

Chapter 26 — Job Sites & Profession Control

  • Job blocks: Place job site blocks to assign professions (Lectern, Smithing Table, etc.).
  • Re-rolling trades: Break and replace job blocks before work time to re-roll trades.
  • Trade timing: Trade often to lock trades; cure villagers for permanent discounts where beneficial.
  • Villager safety: Protect villager areas with walls and lighting to prevent raids/bedrock grief.
  • Workday sync: Use bells to gather villagers for management during bulk trade sessions.

Chapter 27 — Trading Halls & Efficient Layouts

  • Hall layout: Keep one workstation type per booth with a trading window for quick access.
  • XP loop: Funnel trades that consume resources you produce (e.g., sticks → fletcher emeralds).
  • Curing strategy: Curing zombie villagers yields large permanent discounts if performed safely.
  • Villager breeding: Control doors/capacity and food to scale the population predictably.
  • Overflow handling: Use minecarts or water streams to move villagers into storage cells.

Chapter 28 — Iron & Golem Economies

  • Iron farms: Use zombie-villager panic mechanics to spawn Iron Golems for automatic iron drops.
  • Kill design: Funnel golems into safe damage zones (lava/killing chamber) for collection.
  • Redundancy: Build multi-cell systems to keep constant iron output under load.
  • Processing: Auto-smelt and compress ingots into blocks for storage efficiency.
  • Maintenance: Watch villager AI updates after patches; re-test farm logic after version changes.

Chapter 29 — Farming for Emeralds

  • Crop trades: Farmers trade crops for emeralds — automate crop farms for steady emerald flow.
  • Composter leverage: Excess crops → composters → bone meal to speed throughput when needed.
  • Stick trades: Fletchers can buy sticks in bulk for emeralds — use wooden plank conversion funnels.
  • Experience farming: Combine villager trading with XP farms for enchant/repair cycles.
  • Trade rotation: Keep villagers unemployed until desired trade appears, then lock it by trading once.

Chapter 30 — Bell, Raid Management & Safety

  • Bell usage: Use bells to gather villagers into secure chambers during raids.
  • Raid platforms: Build isolated platforms for raid capture and automation of drops.
  • Totem farms: Automate raid waves to generate Totems of Undying through controlled kill setups.
  • Reinforcement: Layer perimeter defenses (walls, trenches, lighting) for village safety.
  • Recovery: Keep a simple bed + emergency chest near the village for rapid respawn and repair tasks.

Phase VII: Dimensional Logistics & Endgame (Chapters 31–35)

Portal math, endgame loot acquisition, and logistics to traverse and exploit multiple dimensions.

Chapter 31 — Nether Portal Math

  • Coordinate conversion: Nether X/Z = Overworld X/Z ÷ 8 — use to align fast travel. :contentReference[oaicite:5]{index=5}
  • Precise placement: Calculate portal positions and build safe arrival rooms on both sides.
  • Portal linking: Ensure Y coordinate differences are accounted for when terrain is irregular.
  • Hub design: Create a nether hub with labeled portals and rail connections for continent links.
  • Portal defense: Encapsulate Nether-side portals in blast-proof boxes to prevent ghast access.

Chapter 32 — Shulker Box Inventory Scaling

  • Shulker craft: 2 shulker shells + chest → shulker box; preserves contents on break. :contentReference[oaicite:6]{index=6}
  • Color-coding: Dye shulker boxes and label by resource category for fast sorting.
  • Swap systems: Use piston-swaps for full/empty shulker box exchanges in auto-storage.
  • Exploration rule: Carry at least 2 empty shulker boxes on long excursions for loot consolidation.
  • Chest integration: Keep shulker boxes in dedicated storage rows for chest-based retrieval systems.

Chapter 33 — Elytra & Flight Logistics

  • Location: Elytra are found in End ships in the treasure room — secure the End City first. :contentReference[oaicite:7]{index=7}
  • Rocket support: Carry firework rockets (paper + gunpowder) for powered flight and long-distance travel.
  • Durability management: Enchant Elytra with Unbreaking + Mending to extend lifespan.
  • Docking: Build safe landing pads and small End bases to store rockets and repair rigs.
  • Training: Practice low-altitude takes and glide arcs before going for long jumps.

Chapter 34 — Conduit & Ocean Operations

  • Conduit craft: Heart of the Sea + Prismarine frame → Conduit; grants underwater benefits when activated.
  • Construction: Build conduit hearts inside prismarine frames; activate with sufficient prismarine blocks.
  • Monument runs: Use invisibility & milk to bypass guardians; milk removes mining fatigue from Elder Guardians.
  • Water works: Use conduits for sustained underwater builds and monument harvesting.
  • Logistics: Store sponges and drying setups for repeated monument clearing operations.

Chapter 35 — End Crystal & Dragon Runs

  • End crystal craft: Glass + ghast tear + eye of ender — necessary to respawn Ender Dragon.
  • Dragon tactics: Destroy crystals first, then use beds/explosives for damage if desired.
  • Practice runs: Do multiple practice runs with enchanted gear and potions before final attempts.
  • Loot recovery: Secure Dragon's Breath and elytra from ships after victory.
  • Post-fight: Use end gateways to reach outer islands and gather end-city loot safely.

Phase X: The Technical Singularity (Chapters 46–50)

World-scale automation, logistics, and extreme engineering — plan backups and deploy in phases.

Chapter 46 — Rail Network Synthesis

  • Rail basics: Iron + sticks → rails; powered rails use gold + redstone for speed control.
  • Station layout: Create inbound/outbound lanes and buffering carts with hopper loaders.
  • Automation: Use detector rails to trigger loading/unloading via redstone or droppers.
  • Efficiency: Use ice highways in the Nether to supplement rail networks across continents.
  • Fail-safe: Build bypass routes and manual switches for maintenance periods.

Chapter 47 — Ice Highways & Boat Travel

  • Blue ice: Use Blue Ice + boat in tunnels for fastest horizontal travel (best in Nether for long links).
  • Tunnel safety: Light and wall tunnels to prevent mob interference and portal cross-linking.
  • Hub policy: Map hub nodes and label directions in a central nav board.
  • Maintenance: Use protected glass corridors to prevent accidental breaks.
  • Integration: Combine ice highways with portal endpoints to reach remote railheads faster.

Chapter 48 — Shulker Sorter Logic & Full-Box Handling

  • Detection: Use comparators to detect full shulker boxes and swap them automatically with pistons.
  • Rotation: Build carousel systems that rotate full boxes into collection chests and return empties.
  • Throughput: Design for predicted max intake rates; add parallel lanes to avoid chokepoints.
  • Monitoring: Add visual indicators (redstone lamps) for full/overflow conditions.
  • Recovery: Include manual access points to clear jams quickly.

Chapter 49 — Map Art Canvas Preparation

  • Canvas size: 1 map = 128×128 blocks; flatten and mark coordinates beforehand.
  • Palette: Use concrete/terracotta/planks for consistent map colors.
  • Templates: Use small-scale mockups and coordinates grid for placement accuracy.
  • Locking maps: Use cartography table to lock maps and avoid accidental overwriting.
  • Showcase: Build gallery spaces with frames and lighting to preserve pieces.

Chapter 50 — World Eater Recipe (Extreme Engineering)

  • Flying machine design: Use TNT, observers, pistons, and slime blocks only after testing in backups.
  • Chunk edits: Plan phased removal to avoid server lag and accidental structure loss.
  • Backup policy: Snapshot world saves before any large-scale mechanical or TNT-driven operations.
  • Rollback plan: Keep a secondary restore copy and a stepwise deconstruction checklist.
  • Ethics: On multiplayer servers, always coordinate with admins and players before destructive projects.
